## Summary Assessment

The domain-deepening corpus is substantially stronger than a typical policy research collection. The majority of files are publication-ready with minor additions. The files built on prior research threads (criminal justice, anti-corruption, digital government) are exceptional. The weakest files are not bad — they are merely adequate where others are excellent. There are three clear tiers.

**Broad pattern across the corpus**: Updated data is consistently the strongest dimension. International benchmarks and fiscal estimates are the most variable. Counterargument engagement is strong in about half the files and perfunctory in the other half. Actionable intelligence is strong where the domain has identifiable pressure points; thin where reform requires constitutional or legislative change with long time horizons.

**The single most important gap across domains**: Several files have excellent supply-side evidence (what the problem is, what reform looks like) but thin demand-side political economy analysis (who specifically blocks reform, what their veto mechanism is, what has shifted their position historically). For a proposal aiming at publication and use by organizers, this is the deepening that would add the most value.

## Cross-Domain Observations

**Structural gap 1 — Political economy is the weak dimension across the corpus.**
Nearly every file has strong evidence on what the problem is and what reform looks like. Far fewer files systematically analyze who specifically blocks reform, what their institutional veto point is, and what has historically shifted their position. The anti-corruption file (revolving door natural experiment, PIN collapse), the campaign finance file (doctrinal chain), and the fiscal reform file (1986 Tax Reform Act coalition analysis) are the exceptions. The others need a "why this hasn't passed" section that is as rigorous as the "what the evidence shows" section.

**Structural gap 2 — Implementation failure modes are underrepresented.**
Several international benchmarks are presented as success cases without adequate treatment of the failure modes or adaptation requirements. The notable exceptions: domain-03-democratic-participation.md's Irish assembly failure case, domain-04-digital-government.md's India Aadhaar exclusion crisis, and domain-09-federalism.md's Colorado River cautionary design case. Every file citing a positive international benchmark should identify at least one jurisdiction where the same reform failed and why.

**Structural gap 3 — The 2025-2026 rollback is documented inconsistently across files.**
Some files (anti-corruption, digital government, infrastructure, judicial independence, rights protection) have excellent incident-level documentation of the current administration's specific actions. Others (housing, labor, economic concentration) treat the current period primarily through the lens of pre-existing structural problems without documenting the specific 2025-2026 changes that have accelerated them. For a proposal arguing that this is an urgent crisis requiring immediate response, the incident-level 2025-2026 documentation needs to be consistent across all domains.

**Structural gap 4 — The cross-domain synthesis is present only in the proposal, not in the companion files.**
The proposal's theory of change (Section 5.4's 13 reinforcing feedback loops) argues that piecemeal reform underperforms. But the companion files are largely siloed: each makes the case for its domain in isolation. The strongest exception is domain-04-digital-government.md, which explicitly identifies itself as foundational for five other domains. A brief cross-reference section in each file identifying which other domains it reinforces and which domains it depends on would make the corpus more useful as an integrated argument rather than a collection of separate evidence bases.

**Strongest files — common characteristics:**
The Tier 1 files share: (a) specific numbers throughout with methodology acknowledged, (b) contested findings engaged rather than elided, (c) at least one failure case that makes the success cases more credible, (d) incident-level 2025-2026 documentation, and (e) actionable pressure points. The template for deepening the Tier 2 and 3 files is visible in the Tier 1 files.

**Most under-utilized evidence across the corpus:**
The V-Dem 0.75→0.57 decline figure from judicial-independence-evidence.md is a headline number that belongs in the proposal's Part I and in multiple other files' executive overviews. No other number in the corpus so precisely quantifies the democratic backsliding claim that the entire proposal is built on. It should be pulled forward wherever the case for urgency is being made.
