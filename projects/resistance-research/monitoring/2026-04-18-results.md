## 1. Ballroom / Leon — Branch A Confirmed, New Stay Clock Running

### What Is Confirmed

**Leon's April 16 clarification order** (confirmed across PBS NewsHour, Washington Times, KSAT/AP, The Hill, KPBS/AP):

Leon issued the clarification the D.C. Circuit remand required, but came down firmly against the administration's overbroad reading of his security exception. His ruling:

- Above-ground ballroom construction: **halted**, except work required to cover or secure that portion of the site.
- Below-ground work: **permitted** — excavations, bunkers, military installations, medical facilities below the ballroom footprint may proceed.
- Administration's argument that the entire ballroom project "from tip to tail" falls within the security exception: **rejected explicitly**. Leon wrote: "That is neither a reasonable nor a correct reading of my Order!"
- Key line: "The President of the United States is the steward of the White House for future generations of First Families. He is not, however, the owner."
- Key line: "National security is not a blank check to proceed with otherwise unlawful activity."

Leon stayed his new order for **one week** — running to approximately April 23-24 — to allow the administration time to seek appellate or Supreme Court review.

**Administration's response** (confirmed same-day, April 16):

The administration filed a notice of appeal to the D.C. Circuit. This is a notice of intent, not an emergency motion; it initiates the appellate track but does not by itself stay the order.

Trump publicly criticized the ruling on April 17, consistent with ongoing political messaging about the case.

**What is NOT confirmed as of April 18**:

- No SCOTUS docket entry for *National Trust for Historic Preservation v. GSA* has been confirmed in indexed search results. Whether the administration will file at SCOTUS during the seven-day stay window (by approximately April 23-24) remains unconfirmed.
- National Trust contempt motion: not filed, because contempt scenario (Branch C) did not materialize. No construction defiance record created.
- The D.C. Circuit has not yet acted on the administration's notice of appeal.

### Analytical Note

The Branch C contempt scenario the April 17-preview brief assessed as most likely did not materialize. Leon acted, resolving the deadline cleanly. The outcome is better for the National Trust than Branch C would have been in one respect — a clean court order is more durable than a contempt-fight record — but it also means the administration now has a seven-day stay window to seek SCOTUS relief, extending the litigation.

The Rao dissent (arguing the majority should have vacated Leon outright on national security grounds) remains the administration's strongest argument for a SCOTUS administrative stay. Roberts's question at the IEEPA oral argument about statutes that "prove too much" suggests some skepticism of overbroad executive emergency authority, but the White House executive-mansion authority question is distinct from IEEPA and gives the administration a different constitutional hook. SCOTUS action during the seven-day stay window is the key unknown through April 23-24.

### Escalation Status: AMBER (downgraded from CODE RED)

The immediate contempt risk resolved. New watch clock: April 23-24 stay expiry. If no SCOTUS administrative stay is obtained by then, Leon's above-ground halt reinstates and the contempt scenario reactivates.
