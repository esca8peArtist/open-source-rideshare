## 1. Ballroom / Leon — DC Circuit Stay Through June 5, Construction Resumed

**DC Circuit stay order, April 18 (Friday evening)**:

The three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit issued its order on the evening of April 18, the same day the April 18 brief was filed. The order stayed Leon's April 16 clarification order, which had halted above-ground construction. The effect: above-ground ballroom construction may proceed pending further proceedings.

Confirmed from multiple sources (NBC News, The Hill, ENR, Fortune, Daily Record, KVIA/AP):

- Leon's injunction is on hold. Construction — above-ground and below-ground — is permitted to continue.
- The panel scheduled oral argument for **June 5** to hear whether construction should be halted during the appeal.
- The panel: **Judges Patricia Millett, Neomi Rao, and Bradley Garcia**.

**Construction status**: Confirmed proceeding as of April 18 evening. No contempt record has been created because the circuit stay preceded any construction defiance of Leon's order.

**SCOTUS**: No confirmed SCOTUS filing. With the circuit stay through June 5, there is no administration need to file at SCOTUS for now.

**National Trust / contempt**: No contempt motion filed. The contempt scenario did not materialize at any point in the April 18–23 window.

## 6. New Major Development: DC Circuit Ends Boasberg Contempt Probe (April 14)

### What Is Confirmed

This development was not indexed in the April 18 brief. It is confirmed from Washington Post, CNN, The Hill, Fox News, and multiple wire sources.

On **April 14**, a divided DC Circuit panel ordered Chief District Judge James Boasberg to terminate his criminal contempt inquiry into senior Trump administration officials over the March 2025 deportation of Venezuelan migrants to El Salvador under the Alien Enemies Act.

**The majority**: Judges Neomi Rao and Justin Walker (both Trump appointees) ruled that Boasberg's contempt inquiry constituted an "unwarranted impairment" of the executive branch and represented an abuse of discretion. They described the investigation as "intrusive" of national security and foreign policy deliberations.

**The dissent**: Judge Michelle Childs (Biden appointee) wrote an approximately 80-page dissent arguing that the administration had not met the high bar for appellate intervention in an ongoing district court proceeding, and warning that the majority's ruling "would tie the hands of trial courts to hold contempt proceedings in the future." Key line from the dissent: "Contempt of court is a public offense, and the fate of our democratic republic will depend on whether we treat it as such."

**Legal significance**: This ruling directly limits the ability of district courts to pursue criminal contempt inquiries against executive branch officials for court-defiance in deportation and national security contexts. It is a structural constraint on the contempt mechanism that resistance advocates had considered the primary enforcement tool against court-defying deportation flights. The Rao-Walker majority's reasoning extends beyond this specific case to narrow the contempt power broadly in executive-action contexts.

**Relationship to Abrego Garcia**: The Boasberg ruling is decided on a different procedural posture than the Xinis contempt threat (Xinis's is civil contempt in an injunction-enforcement proceeding; Boasberg's was criminal contempt in a distinct emergency-order context). But the Boasberg ruling gives the administration additional precedent to cite if Xinis attempts formal contempt proceedings after April 28.

### Escalation Status: CODE RED (structural) — the primary contempt enforcement mechanism in the deportation-defiance context has been constrained by circuit precedent. Xinis must now navigate this ruling if she moves toward contempt.
