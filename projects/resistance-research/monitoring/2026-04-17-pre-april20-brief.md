# Pre-April-20 Action Brief: 48-Hour Window
*Written April 17, 2026. This is an action document — what needs to happen in the next 48 hours. For event background, see [2026-04-20-watch.md](2026-04-20-watch.md) and [2026-04-17-actionable-windows.md](2026-04-17-actionable-windows.md).*

## 48-Hour Action Checklist

The window from now through April 19 EOD is the last opportunity to position before April 20 events crystallize. Actions below are ranked by leverage — highest-impact, lowest-barrier first.

**1. Abrego Garcia — read the DOJ brief the moment it drops April 20 morning.**
The single most important document this week. You need to know within hours whether DOJ maintained the Liberia deportation demand, retreated, or filed a simultaneous Fourth Circuit emergency motion. The brief is public. Set a PACER alert for docket number 8:25-cv-00201 (D. Maryland) and a parallel alert on the Fourth Circuit PACER for any new Abrego Garcia filing. If you are an advocate or attorney, this is a same-morning read — the contempt timeline and the April 28 hearing strategy both pivot on what DOJ says.

**2. FISA/702 — make constituent contact calls today, not next week.**
Section 702 expired April 20. The House floor reschedule will happen with limited notice — leadership canceled twice already, which means when it comes back, it comes back fast. The EFF vote tracker identifies the specific House members from competitive districts whose votes on the 212-212 warrant-requirement amendment can swing either way. These are not generic advocacy calls. The script is: "Section 702 just expired. My representative's vote on the warrant requirement amendment is the vote I'm watching heading into November." 5Calls.org has the active campaign framework. Brennan Center and CDT have organizational sign-on letters ready for coordination. Do not wait for a reschedule announcement — that announcement will give you 48 hours and you will be calling cold.

**3. Farm Bill — contact your House member before the last-week-of-April floor vote.**
The House Agriculture Committee passed the bill 34-17 on March 5. Chairman Thompson stated publicly on April 16 that he expects the floor vote before May 1. The bill currently accepts the OBBBA SNAP baseline, meaning it cements a projected 3.2-million coverage reduction rather than reversing it. The window to pressure the seven committee Democrats who voted yes — Jim Costa, Sharice Davids, Don Davis, Adam Gray, Kristen McDonald Rivet, Josh Riley, Gabe Vasquez — is right now, before the floor vote locks positions. FRAC and CBPP have constituent contact infrastructure and talking points. The Senate version is where SNAP restoration is actually possible; the House floor vote is the moment to establish that the Senate must do better.

**4. Document Nebraska Medicaid baseline NOW, before May 1.**
Nebraska work requirements begin May 1 — two weeks out. The June 1 CMS Interim Final Rule comment window is the structural pressure point, but the factual ammunition for that comment period comes from real-time documentation of who loses coverage and why starting May 1. If you are in Nebraska or have contacts with Nebraska legal aid or advocacy organizations (CBPP has in-state partners), begin data-collection protocols now. Case studies, not statistics, will be the lever in comment campaigns and Senate CARE Act negotiations.

**5. Ballroom contempt — monitor East Wing construction site reports April 18 morning.**
The D.C. Circuit stay expired midnight April 17. If no SCOTUS stay was obtained and construction continues April 18, National Trust will file an emergency contempt motion with Judge Leon at court opening. This is photographic contempt — the site is visible from Pennsylvania Avenue. Watch for National Trust press release and Leon's docket. If Branch C materialized (no SCOTUS stay, construction continued), this is the dominant judicial-defiance story entering April 20, and it reframes the Abrego Garcia DOJ brief as part of a coordinated contempt posture across two courts simultaneously.

## Convergence Analysis

The seven threads are not running in parallel. They are compressing.

**FISA expiry and Abrego Garcia brief are the same story told twice.** Both are moments where the administration's claimed authority exceeds what the law actually permits. In Abrego Garcia, DOJ argues the president's foreign affairs prerogative immunizes the executive from court orders about deportation destination. In FISA/702, the executive branch's surveillance apparatus has technically lost statutory authorization but continues operating on a FISC order. The connecting thread is executive power exercised past its legal expiration date — in the immigration context by court order, in the surveillance context by statute. Advocates who understand both can frame them together: this is an administration that governs past the limits set by courts and by Congress.

**The ballroom contempt, if it materialized, changes the Abrego Garcia hearing's political context.** Xinis knows what Leon's courtroom is doing. If the administration is simultaneously in contempt posture before Leon in D.C. and approaching contempt posture before Xinis in Maryland, the April 28 hearing is not a legal technicality — it is a second front in a publicly documented pattern of judicial defiance. Contempt in a visible, photographic, symbolically central location (the White House) makes every subsequent contempt finding easier for the public to understand. This is why the ballroom outcome matters beyond the historic preservation question itself.

**Farm Bill, Medicaid, and May Day are the material consequences of losing the courtroom fights.** The litigation threads are not abstract. The Section 122 tariff regime, if it survives the CIT ruling, continues to collect duties that restructure supply chains and raise costs. OBBBA work requirements, beginning in Nebraska May 1, represent the human cost of the same political project that the judicial resistance threads are contesting. May Day 2026 — which emerged from the March 28 No Kings protests that drew an estimated 8 million participants — is where those material consequences get named and embodied collectively. The convergence is structural: the courts are holding the administration accountable on constitutional authority questions while the policy implementation threads show what happens on the ground when that accountability comes too slowly.

**The AFL-CIO institutional endorsement question is the May Day variable.** Individual union affiliates have endorsed — NEA, SEIU 509 and 2015, National Nurses United, Chicago Teachers Union, AFA-CWA. The AFL-CIO as an institution has not confirmed. An institutional AFL-CIO endorsement before May 1 would be the largest single-day labor escalation since the 1981 PATCO strike in terms of institutional commitment, if not in total number of walkouts. Watch for it. If it comes April 19-20, it reshapes the media landscape entering the April 28 hearing.

## Media and Messaging Moment

April 20 will generate three concurrent stories. Advocates need to decide which one to amplify and how.

**Story one: CAPE launch.** The financial press and trade press will cover this. It is a cooperative compliance mechanism — the government is refunding money. This story does not need amplification from resistance advocates; the importer community has its own voice. What resistance advocates can do is note the contrast: the administration is efficiently refunding $120 billion in invalid tariffs to importers while simultaneously using administrative delay to contest every individual immigration return obligation. The CAPE system proves that when the administration wants to comply with a court order, it can build the infrastructure to do so at scale. The Abrego Garcia "facilitation" claim that compliance is impossible looks different next to a system that processed 26,664 importer enrollments.

**Story two: DOJ Abrego Garcia brief.** This is the most legally consequential document of the week and will receive serious press coverage. The framing question for advocates: is this a story about one man's case, or is it a story about whether the executive branch is bound by federal courts at all? The Liberia demand — sending a Maryland man with no connection to Liberia to Liberia, despite a Costa Rica agreement that would satisfy the return obligation, while simultaneously prosecuting him in Tennessee — is not a close legal question. It is a deliberate choice to construct an impossible compliance situation. That is the story. Xinis has already called it "a fantasy" on the record. The DOJ brief either retreats from the fantasy or doubles down. Either outcome is news.

**Story three: FISA expiry + reform window.** This is the story the surveillance reform movement has been waiting for since the 212-212 tie in 2024. The statutory authority has lapsed. The reform coalition has a vehicle (SAFE Act, Durbin-Lee), a historical precedent (the 212-212 vote showed the warrant requirement can pass), and a mobilized constituency. The message is simple: Congress let this expire because it could not answer a basic question about whether the FBI needs a warrant to search Americans' communications. The answer should be yes. That question is now live on the floor the moment leadership reschedules.

**Recommended framing for advocates covering all three:** "April 20 is the day the administration's theory of power gets tested in three different institutions simultaneously — the courts, the customs system, and Congress. In every case, the question is the same: is the executive branch bound by the rules everyone else has to follow?"

## Watch List: April 21-28 Cascade

**April 21-22: Ballroom contempt proceedings, if Branch C materialized.** Leon's response to a National Trust contempt motion will set the pace for the Xinis contempt timeline. If Leon moves quickly — Order to Show Cause within 48 hours — it signals that federal district judges are prepared to use their contempt authority without waiting for appellate permission. If Leon moves cautiously — scheduling a hearing weeks out — it signals that even willing judges are absorbing the administration's attrition strategy.

**April 21-25: Crenshaw/Nashville ruling or Blanche subpoena.** The Nashville dismissal is long overdue on any normal drafting timeline. A ruling in this window — before the April 28 Xinis hearing — could be timed deliberately to clarify the landscape. A Blanche subpoena would dominate both proceedings and every news cycle simultaneously.

**April 21-25: FISA floor reschedule announcement.** After the expiry, leadership faces pressure to reschedule quickly to restore statutory authority. The 48-hour action window from any reschedule announcement is the moment constituent calls are highest-leverage. Watch for House Rules Committee procedural scheduling notices.

**April 24-25: May Day organizing finalization.** The April 29 national lead-up events are the preview indicator for May 1 scale. Confirmed participation count from AFL-CIO affiliates and any new institutional endorsements landing this week will forecast whether May Day 2026 reaches the scale advocates are projecting.

**April 28: Xinis hearing — the week's culminating event.** The hearing will be shaped by everything that happened in the preceding eight days: whether DOJ's brief showed any compliance path, whether the Nashville prosecution was dismissed, whether the ballroom contempt proceeded, and whether the May Day mobilization is building sufficient political pressure to make continued defiance costly. The hearing is not just a legal proceeding — it is a public record of how far the administration will go and what the courts will do about it. Watch the transcript, not just the ruling.

**Watch for Section 122 CIT ruling — any day through May.** The panel has been deliberating since April 10. A ruling invalidating Section 122 would open a second major refund stream while the CAPE Phase 1 refund process is already running — simultaneously demonstrating SCOTUS's IEEPA ruling was correct and forcing the administration to either comply with a second major tariff loss or escalate its appeal posture. This is the background thread most likely to break unexpectedly.
