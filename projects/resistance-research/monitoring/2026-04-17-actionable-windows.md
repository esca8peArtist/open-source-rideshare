## 1. Section 702 / FISA Surveillance Law

### Current Status

Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act **expired at its statutory sunset on April 20, 2026**. Congress was unable to pass reauthorization before the deadline. The House Rules Committee advanced a procedural rule for an 18-month "clean" extension (H.R. 8035) but House GOP leadership twice canceled the floor vote — first on April 16, then again as of April 17 — amid intra-party divisions between surveillance hawks (who wanted clean extension) and privacy-oriented conservatives and progressive Democrats (who demanded warrant requirements for US person queries).

**The practical consequence of expiration:** A prior FISC annual recertification of the 702 program, granted in March 2026, means that collection continues under that order until it expires in approximately March 2027. The program has not gone dark. However, the statutory authority has technically lapsed, and any new collection certifications require fresh legislation. Intel community officials, including CIA Director Ratcliffe, were warning of "blind spots" as the expiration approached, citing Iran ceasefire intelligence needs.

**Reform context:** In April 2024, a warrant-requirement amendment to the last reauthorization tied 212-212 in the House — one vote short of passage. That same cross-partisan coalition (privacy-conservative Republicans plus progressive Democrats) has been demanding a warrant requirement as the price of reauthorization in 2026. The bipartisan SAFE Act (Durbin-Lee, introduced February 2026) includes the warrant requirement and created a Senate vehicle.

### Deadline

The FISC recertification order likely runs until approximately March 2027. That is the de facto new deadline for legislation. The statutory lapse, however, creates a short legislative window before the Senate or House returns to this: momentum for reform is highest immediately after expiration, when civil liberties advocates are mobilized and the 212-212 precedent is maximally salient.

### What's at Stake

- Whether the FBI must obtain a warrant before querying Section 702 data for US persons — the central reform question since the FBI's 3.4 million US-person queries in 2021 were documented
- PCLOB independence provisions, which can be attached to the reauthorization — PCLOB is non-operational following Trump administration removals
- The 2026 legislative posture is the best opportunity since 2024 for the warrant requirement to pass, because the lapse has disrupted the "just clean-extend" equilibrium

### Action Items

1. **Constituent pressure on the two swing-vote House members** who tipped the 212-212 tie in 2024. EFF maintains a vote tracker identifying the specific members from competitive districts who voted against the warrant amendment. Constituent contact campaigns now, before any floor reschedule, are highest-leverage.
2. **Senate Judiciary Committee hearings on SAFE Act** — these are scheduled. Organizational sign-on letters for the warrant requirement and for PCLOB independence provisions should be coordinated through Brennan Center and CDT advocacy networks now.
3. **Monitor for House floor reschedule** — the week of April 27, or after the May 1 recess. Any reschedule announcement is a 48-hour action window. 5Calls.org has an active campaign script.

## 2. SNAP and Food Security — OBBBA Implementation

### Current Status

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21), enacted in 2025, restructures SNAP through three mechanisms that phase in over FY2027:

- **Administrative cost shift:** Federal share drops from ~50% to 25% starting FY2027, giving states a strong fiscal incentive to reduce caseloads
- **Work requirement expansion:** Extended to adults through age 54 (previously 49)
- **Parent work requirements:** Reinstated for parents of children 7 and older

CBO estimates 3.2 million people will lose SNAP eligibility by 2034 as a direct result.

**The FY2027 appropriations window is more immediately actionable than the legislation itself.** The 50/50-to-25/75 administrative cost shift takes effect in FY2027 — which begins October 1, 2026. The Agriculture appropriations subcommittee markup happens May-September 2026. An appropriations rider that delays or modifies the FY2027 cost ratio shift can prevent the caseload-reduction pressure before it begins.

**Restoration legislation:** The Restoring Food Security for American Families and Farmers Act (introduced in the 119th Congress, supported by FRAC, CBPP, and approximately 1,500 organizations) calls for repeal of the SNAP provisions. As of April 2026, it has not passed. Its most immediate strategic value is cosponsor list building and discharge petition potential.

### Deadline

- **May-September 2026:** FY2027 Agriculture appropriations markup — the window for a rider delaying the administrative cost shift
- **October 1, 2026:** FY2027 begins — administrative cost shift takes effect unless reversed
- **September 30, 2026:** Current Farm Bill extension expires; any new Farm Bill must take a position on the OBBBA SNAP baseline

### What's at Stake

The OBBBA SNAP provisions function as a slow-acting defunding mechanism. States facing a 25/75 cost split will have structural incentives to tighten work requirement verification, reduce outreach, and shrink caseloads — all without requiring any new legislative action. The FY2027 appropriations rider is the only near-term mechanism to interrupt this before it begins. Once the cost shift takes effect, reversing it requires new legislation in a more difficult political environment.

### Action Items

1. **Agriculture appropriations subcommittee members** — particularly members with large SNAP recipient constituencies. The subcommittee markup is the moment to insert a cost-ratio rider. Contact window: now through August 2026.
2. **Farm Bill floor debate** (last week of April) — the Farm Bill must address the OBBBA baseline. The House bill (Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026) currently accepts the OBBBA SNAP baseline rather than reversing it. Senate Agriculture Committee members from red and purple states with high rural SNAP usage are the targets for a restoration provision in the Senate version.
3. **Cosponsor the Restoring Food Security Act** — organizations should coordinate letter campaigns on House and Senate Agriculture Committee members who have not signed on.

## 3. Farm Bill Reauthorization

### Current Status

The House Agriculture Committee passed the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026 on March 5 with a 34-17 bipartisan vote (all Republicans + 7 Democrats). Chairman Thompson (R-PA) stated publicly on April 16 that he is confident the bill reaches the House floor before May 1. The House Rules Committee signaled it could schedule floor consideration the last week of April.

**Caution flags from inside the House:** Senior House GOP leadership staff have privately warned the bill is "in big trouble" on the floor due to intra-party divides over pesticide labeling preemption (a California delegation priority) and state-level livestock law provisions. A floor vote could be bumped to after the May 1 recess.

**The Senate path is separate and harder.** The Senate Agriculture Committee has not yet produced a parallel bill. Senate passage will require 60 votes, meaning at least 7 Republican senators must join a unified Democratic caucus — or the bill must satisfy enough Republican agriculture-state priorities to advance with a majority of Republicans. The September 30 deadline creates genuine urgency, but Farm Bills have lapsed before (the 2018 bill technically expired before the 2018 lapse extension passed).

**OBBBA complication:** The OBBBA modified SNAP outside the Farm Bill process. Any new Farm Bill must either accept the OBBBA SNAP cuts as the new baseline or explicitly reverse them — a politically loaded decision that will delay consensus.

### Deadline

**September 30, 2026** — current extension expires. If no new Farm Bill passes, Congress will face a fourth extension vote, likely as part of a continuing resolution.

### What's at Stake

The Farm Bill controls commodity programs (price supports for corn, wheat, soybeans, cotton), crop insurance, conservation programs (CRP, EQIP), rural development funding, and — most significantly for resistance-research purposes — SNAP. A Farm Bill that accepts the OBBBA baseline cements the 3.2-million projected coverage loss. A Farm Bill that restores prior SNAP parameters would represent the most significant near-term reversal of the OBBBA food security cuts.

### Action Items

1. **House floor vote, last week of April** — contact your House member. The seven Democrats who voted for the House committee bill represent districts where the OBBBA SNAP restoration debate is live: Jim Costa (CA-21), Sharice Davids (KS-03), Don Davis (NC-01), Adam Gray (CA-13), Kristen McDonald Rivet (MI-08), Josh Riley (NY-19), Gabe Vasquez (NM-02).
2. **Senate Agriculture Committee outreach** — prioritize Republican senators from expansion states who have expressed concern about rural food access: Collins (ME), Murkowski (AK), Capito (WV), Fischer (NE).
3. **NSAC (National Sustainable Agriculture Coalition), FRAC, and CBPP** are the primary Farm Bill advocacy organizations running constituent contact infrastructure.

## 4. Healthcare — Medicaid Work Requirements and ACA Subsidies

### 4a. Medicaid Work Requirements — Nebraska Begins May 1

**Nebraska goes live May 1, 2026** — the first state to implement OBBBA work requirements, eight months ahead of the federal January 1, 2027 deadline. An estimated 30,000 Nebraskans will be subject to the 80-hours/month requirement. Hospitals in Nebraska are already warning of care disruption and staffing strain (reported April 13).

Montana is implementing in July 2026. Both states are operating under state plan amendments — no 1115 waiver required under the OBBBA authority.

**Critical upcoming deadlines:**

| Deadline | Event |
|---|---|
| May 1, 2026 | Nebraska work requirements begin |
| June 1, 2026 | CMS must issue Interim Final Rule on work requirements |
| June 30–Aug 31, 2026 | States must conduct mandatory member outreach |
| July 2026 | Montana work requirements begin |
| December 31, 2026 | States must shift to semi-annual Medicaid eligibility redeterminations (from annual) |
| January 1, 2027 | All expansion states must implement work requirements (federal deadline) |

**The June 1 Interim Final Rule is the most actionable near-term pressure point.** CMS is legally required to issue an interim final rule by June 1 that defines implementation standards, exemption categories, and verification requirements. The APA notice-and-comment process applies, creating a public comment window. Organized comment campaigns coordinated through Georgetown CCF, CBPP, and NASHP can shape the exemption architecture that will determine how many people lose coverage in practice.

**CBO impact figures:**
- 4.8 million people projected to lose Medicaid coverage from work requirements
- 11.8 million total projected to lose coverage from all OBBBA Medicaid provisions
- $1.02 trillion in reduced federal Medicaid and CHIP spending over 10 years

### 4b. ACA Enhanced Premium Tax Credits — Senate CARE Act Pending

Enhanced PTCs expired December 31, 2025. The House passed a 3-year extension on January 8, 2026 (230-196, with 17 Republicans). The Senate bipartisan CARE Act — led by a small group finalizing compromise terms — would restore enhanced PTCs for 2 years with income caps and minimum premiums added.

**Without restoration:** 2026 marketplace premiums have already risen an average of 114% for subsidized enrollees (from $888 to $1,904 average annual premium). CBO estimates 4.8 million additional uninsured by 2034.

**Senate vehicle:** The CARE Act is a bipartisan compromise. The Senate Finance Committee Republicans from high-enrollment states (Collins/ME, Murkowski/AK, Tillis/NC, Capito/WV) are the primary targets for constituent pressure. Their constituents' enrollment numbers are documented by KFF district-level enrollment data.

### Action Items

1. **Submit comments on the CMS Interim Final Rule** when it is released (by June 1). Georgetown CCF and CBPP will publish comment toolkits. Priority: broad exemption categories, simplified verification processes, and continuation-of-care protections for people who miss monthly documentation deadlines.
2. **Contact Senate Finance Committee members** on the CARE Act — specifically Republican members from states with above-average marketplace enrollment. The KFF district enrollment data is the factual weapon.
3. **Document Nebraska implementation** beginning May 1 — concrete, real-time evidence of who loses coverage and why will be the most persuasive data for Senate ACA negotiations and for the January 2027 federal Medicaid rollout debate.

### Alaska RCV Repeal — November 3, 2026

Alaska's top-four RCV system, which produced the most watched RCV elections in the country (the 2022 and 2024 federal cycles), faces a repeal initiative that qualified for the November 3 ballot. The 2024 defeat of the previous repeal was by 0.23% — the narrowest ballot measure result in Alaska's history. Repeal supporters collected approximately 48,000 signatures for the 2026 initiative.

A defense organization, Protect Alaska's Elections, has registered intent to spend. In 2024, the defense campaign spent $15 million. FairVote and RepresentUs are expected to engage nationally.

**This is the highest-priority defensive action in electoral reform for 2026.** If Alaska's system is repealed, it removes the most prominent RCV model in the country and provides a national template for repeal campaigns in other states.

### Georgia HB 1322 — Assembly Felonization

Georgia House Bill 1322 (2026) would reclassify "riot" from a misdemeanor to a felony carrying 1-20 years in prison. Georgia's existing riot definition is broad enough to encompass any protest where any single participant causes property damage — meaning an individual bad actor's conduct can expose an entire organized demonstration to felony liability. ACLU of Georgia has testified this would make organizations rationally unwilling to support mass mobilization.

**Legislative session status:** Monitor Georgia legislative calendar. If HB 1322 passes and is signed, it would be the most significant anti-assembly legislation enacted in 2026 and would be subject to First Amendment challenge.

### Illinois BIPA — Spring 2026 Legislative Session

Industry efforts to weaken the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) are introduced every legislative session. BIPA is the structural model for biometric privacy protection nationally — its weakening removes the primary litigation incentive driving tech company compliance. The spring 2026 Illinois session is the current window. EPIC, ACLU of Illinois, and BIPA class-action litigation firms are the active defenders.

**Action:** Monitor Illinois legislature for any BIPA amendment bills. Constituent contact to Illinois lawmakers from civil liberties and labor constituencies is the intervention.

## 6. FY2027 NDAA — Defense Policy Window

### Current Status

Based on the FY2026 NDAA cycle (subcommittee markups July 8-9 2025, HASC full markup July 15), the FY2027 cycle will follow approximately the same schedule: **subcommittee markups beginning May-June 2026, full committee markup in July.** This is the annual guaranteed-floor-vote legislative vehicle for defense accountability and war powers provisions.

### Priority provisions for FY2027 advocacy

- **2001 AUMF repeal/sunset** (H.R. 6751, Jayapal-Massie-McGovern-Griffith-Casar-Crane): The 2001 AUMF has been used to justify operations in 22 countries. The enacted FY2026 NDAA repealed the 2002 Iraq AUMF and 1991 Gulf War AUMF — establishing that AUMF repeal via NDAA is procedurally achievable. The 2001 AUMF repeal needs House floor pressure; the Senate vehicle is a Kaine-led NDAA amendment. Conference in November-December 2026 is where these tracks converge.
- **Audit the Pentagon Act** (H.R. 7555, Pocan-Biggs): 0.5% budget return for a failed audit. Pentagon will fail its 9th consecutive audit in December 2026. The 9th failure, using the "eight consecutive failures" organizing frame, is a media hook for FY2027 NDAA advocacy.
- **Section 702 warrant amendment via NDAA:** If FISA reauthorization does not pass as standalone legislation before the NDAA conference window, a warrant-requirement provision can potentially be attached to the NDAA intelligence authorities section.

### Action Items

1. **May-June 2026: Contact HASC and SASC subcommittee members** on 2001 AUMF repeal and Pentagon audit accountability. Win Without War, FCNL, and Quincy Institute maintain action alert infrastructure.
2. **July 2026: Full committee markup** — the moment for floor amendment preparation and member whipping.
3. **Frame the December 2026 audit announcement** — "9th consecutive failure" — as the NDAA media moment for 2027 cycle.

## 7. Other Active Windows

### TANF Reauthorization — December 31, 2026

TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families) is authorized through December 31, 2026 under P.L. 119-75 (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026). TANF has been run on short-term extensions since its 2010 expiration — Congress last produced a substantive reauthorization in 2005. The funding level has remained at $16.5 billion annually since the 1990s with no inflation adjustment.

This deadline is less urgent than Farm Bill/SNAP because Congress will almost certainly pass another extension rather than allow lapse. The advocacy priority is seizing the reauthorization debate to modernize the funding formula. H.R. 2584 (Protect TANF Resources for Families Act) and the JOBS for Success Act are the live legislative vehicles.

**Action:** Push Senate Finance and House Ways and Means for a substantive multi-year reauthorization rather than another short-term extension. Build on the Arizona, Iowa, Nebraska, Ohio, Virginia pilot program results from the Family Reconciliation Act experiments launched September 2025.

### CTC Refundability — 2026 Tax Legislation Vehicle

The OBBBA increased the Child Tax Credit to $2,200 but did not expand refundability. An estimated 19 million children — 1 in 4 children under 17 — receive less than the full credit or nothing. The SSN requirement added by OBBBA removed approximately 4.5 million children.

**The vehicle:** Any tax legislation in 2026 — including any second reconciliation package or TCJA extension debates — creates an opportunity for CTC refundability expansion. The Tax Relief for American Families and Workers Act (H.R. 7024, 357-70 House vote in 2024) is the established bipartisan template. Economic Security Project coordinates CTC advocacy.

### EU Anti-SLAPP Directive — December 2026 Implementation

The EU Anti-SLAPP Directive (Directive 2024/1069) requires EU member states to implement early dismissal mechanisms for SLAPP suits by December 2026. US companies with EU operations are currently modifying litigation policies to comply. This creates a corporate constituency for the US SPEAK FREE Act: corporate legal and government affairs staff at multinationals with EU presence have a concrete business reason (reduced dual compliance burden) to support federal anti-SLAPP legislation. Congressional staff working on SPEAK FREE Act should be targeting this constituency now.

### Humphrey's Executor (Trump v. Slaughter) — Decision by June 2026

SCOTUS is expected to rule before the end of its June 2026 term on whether to overrule or substantially narrow Humphrey's Executor (1935), which limits presidential removal authority over independent agency heads. Oral argument (December 8, 2025) strongly suggested the conservative supermajority will overrule or narrow. A ruling would give the president at-will removal authority over FTC, NLRB, CFPB, FEC, and MSPB heads.

**Not an actionable window in the traditional sense** — SCOTUS cannot be lobbied. The action context is preparation: organizations dependent on independent agency enforcement (NLRB for labor organizing, FTC for antitrust, CFPB for consumer finance) should be stress-testing what enforcement looks like under fully politicized agency leadership, and building the state-level analog infrastructure now.
